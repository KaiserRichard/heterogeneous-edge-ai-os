## Numbered defects

1. **MINOR — pages 9 and 15: requested timing values are presented too much like measured timing.** Page 9 says E1–E3 are “3×60 s/cell” without “nominal source window”; page 15 places “100 ms inputs” inside the “MEASURED v1” sentence. `docs/STAGE_REPORT.md`, “Setup, run counts, and validity” and “Applied settings,” explicitly treats the 60 s source window and 100 ms source period as **ASSUMED configuration**, and warns that requested periods are not guaranteed under stalls. The completion intervals and age statistics are measured. **Recommended:** say “3 repeats; nominal 60 s source window” and “configured 100 ms input period.” Keep the observed completion intervals under MEASURED. This does not invalidate the queue inference, which is now correctly labelled INFERRED.

2. **MINOR — page 24: the affine clock equation leaves its symbols undefined.** The page identifies the clock domains of T1–T4, but does not define `R`, `O_R`, or `D`, nor name the four timestamp events. The equations reproduce `protocol/CLOCK_SYNC.md`, but a lecturer viewing this backup independently must consult the external document to interpret the map. **Recommended:** add a compact key: R = Pi reference time; O_R = MCU-minus-Pi offset at R; D = dimensionless offset drift; T1/T2 = request send/receive; T3/T4 = reply send/receive. The arithmetic is correct; this is a Q&A readability defect.

3. **MINOR — pages 4–6: central terms are used before their inline explanation.** CPU first appears in the architecture diagram on page 4 and is expanded on page 5. FIFO and OTHER appear in the applied profiles without explaining first-in-first-out real-time scheduling versus ordinary Linux scheduling; FIFO is then also used for a waiting queue. UART and GPIO remain unexpanded. The intended OS audience can follow these, but the referenced checklist asks for definitions at first main-slide use, and the two FIFO meanings are central to the story. **Recommended:** briefly identify `SCHED_OTHER` and `SCHED_FIFO` on page 5 and expand the hardware abbreviations at first use. Avoid adding a glossary slide.
