# R2 — AoI metrics for E3

2026-10-06. Context: `PROJECT.md` §§4–6; `AGENTS.md`; proposed P1/P2 and E3 in `docs/SYSTEM_OVERVIEW.md` §§2–4. **Recommendation:** compare time-average AoI and freshness-threshold violation time as primary metrics; report peak AoI and delivery/drop statistics alongside them. Latest-value buffering alone does not guarantee bounded end-to-end age.

## SOURCE — full-text findings

**[S1] Kaul, Yates, Gruteser, “Status Updates Through Queues,” CISS 2012.** [Full text](https://www.winlab.rutgers.edu/~gruteser/papers/Status%20Updates%20Through%20Queues.pdf), all six pages read.

- §I and §III (pp. 1–3), Fig. 1, Eqs. (3), (8): receiver age increases between useful deliveries and resets to the delivered update's system time. Average AoI is an integral over time, including intervals without deliveries.
- §I and §V (pp. 1, 4): replacing a waiting packet differs from preempting the packet already in service; the latter model retains at most one packet.
- §III-A and §V-A (pp. 3–6), Fig. 2, Eq. (48): the numerical comparison assumes Poisson arrivals and exponential service. Its single-source preemptive result is `1/λ + 1/μ`; the statement about smallest age explicitly assumes independent, memoryless interarrival and service times.

**[S2] Talak, Modiano, “Age-Delay Tradeoffs in Queueing Systems,” IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, accepted manuscript.** [Full text](https://www.mit.edu/~modiano/papers/CV_J_117.pdf), all 17 pages, including appendices, read.

- §II and §II-A (pp. 3–4), Eqs. (1)–(3): packets have independent, identically distributed service times; the model prohibits drops and permits preemption with residual-service resumption. AoI uses the freshest received timestamp, so an out-of-order stale result cannot reduce it.
- §IV-A (p. 6), Lemma 1 and Appendix A (p. 13): with independent renewal intergeneration time `X` and service time `S`, at least one distribution continuous, preemptive LCFS has average age `E[X²]/(2E[X]) + E[min(X,S)]/Pr(S<X)`.
- §III, Theorem 2 (p. 5), §IV-B, Theorem 4 (p. 8): age minimization and delay/variance minimization can conflict. These results optimize service distributions in the stated no-drop model; they do not establish guarantees for our inference pipeline.

Other works cited within these papers are **[unverified]** unless explicitly listed above; their full texts were not read for this ticket.

## IMPLICATION — definitions and reporting contract for E3

**Observation point.** Let `g_i` be the raw sensor/input generation timestamp and `r_i` the time its result becomes available to the MCU control/supervisor decision. Use actuator application time instead if measuring actual actuation, and label that endpoint consistently. Preserve `g_i` through inference, IPC and UART; a result-completion timestamp measures a shorter path. Express both timestamps in an aligned monotonic clock domain.

Define `u(t) = max{g_i : r_i ≤ t}` and **`Δ(t) = t − u(t)`**, following [S1, §I; S2, §II-A]. Index only deliveries that advance `u(t)` as informative updates. Duplicates, old results, heartbeats and failsafe transitions do not reset data AoI. Log those events separately. Delivery latency `r_i − g_i` is only the post-delivery reset value, not the whole AoI process.

For a fixed measurement window `[a,b]`, let `H=b−a`. The following finite-run estimators are our E3 measurement definitions, derived from that age process; they require no arrival/service distribution fit:

| Metric | Definition | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Time-average AoI | `Δ̄_H = (1/H) ∫[a,b] Δ(t) dt` | Overall freshness; includes long inference stalls and dropped updates. |
| Peak AoI | `A_k = Δ(r_k−) = r_k − g_(k−1)` at each informative delivery | Age just before refresh. Report mean, P95/P99 and observed maximum, with peak count. These are event-weighted, not time-weighted. |
| Age violation fraction | `V_H(τ) = (1/H) ∫[a,b] 1{Δ(t)>τ} dt` | Fraction of time the MCU's information exceeds a chosen freshness threshold. It estimates stationary violation probability only under suitable stationarity/ergodicity assumptions. |

**Exact reconstruction.** Between informative deliveries, age rises with slope one. Partition the entire window at these deliveries. For a segment of duration `h` starting at age `d`, its area is `d·h+h²/2`, and its time above `τ` is `min(h,max(0,d+h−τ))`. Sum and divide by `H`; include the final segment even if no further update arrives. Use time-weighted AoI quantiles from the same reconstruction, not quantiles of delivered latencies. A fixed-period MCU sampler is an alternative approximation: disclose its interval and phase.

**Keep denominators explicit.** Also report `(number of peaks > τ)/(number of peaks)` as *peak violation fraction* if useful; it differs from `V_H(τ)`. Define a separate per-input deadline miss fraction: among generated inputs whose `g_i+D` falls within the observation window, count those without a usable result by that deadline, including drops/cancellations. Delivered-only latency exceedances must be labelled conditional on delivery. Report generated, delivered, informative, replaced/cancelled counts and informative inter-delivery gaps.

## IMPLICATION — periodic arrivals and preemption limits

P2 currently overwrites an **unsent result at the bridge**. This resembles replacement in waiting at that stage [S1, §I], not cancellation of inference already running. Linux/FreeRTOS CPU preemption is a separate mechanism: pausing a task does not discard its input. Record replacement location and whether service finishes, resumes or is cancelled; E3 need not change these policies to measure them.

For an ideal single-stage periodic source with period `P`, independent continuous service `S`, immediate service preemption and `p=Pr(S<P)>0`, substituting `X=P` into [S2, §IV-A, Lemma 1] gives `Δ̄=P/2+E[min(P,S)]/p` within its assumptions. This is a sanity model, not a prediction for correlated contention, upstream queues and UART delay. Independently, cancellation every `P` with every inference requiring longer than `P` produces no completions. Even when `0<p<1`, arbitrarily long runs without a completion prevent a deterministic age bound in that ideal model. A small buffer therefore cannot prove H3's proposed bound.

**Campaign choices.** Keep source period, thresholds, run length and load conditions equal for P1/P2. The proposed 30 Hz is an assumption, not a measurement. Choose `τ` and input deadline `D` before comparison; report a threshold sweep if requirements remain TBD. Heartbeat timeouts are not automatically freshness thresholds. Repeat runs and report uncertainty across runs rather than treating correlated samples as independent. Record clock alignment error; if bounded by `ε`, bracket violation time using reconstructed thresholds `τ+ε` and `τ−ε`. Use a declared warm-up with known initial `u(a)`; otherwise report the no-initial-data interval separately, never initialize it to zero age. Include stalls through run end; if no informative delivery occurs, peak statistics are undefined while known-initial-age integrals remain measurable.
